## 2. Registry rules

1. **Founder only.** Only Chais Hill may register a coin. No other person may submit, propose, or enter a coin on his behalf without his express instruction for that coin.
2. **One entry per coin.** Each coin has exactly one registry entry. A coin is identified by its name and ticker together; a second entry for the same coin is a duplicate, not a new coin.
3. **No duplicates.** Before a new entry is recorded, the registry is checked for an existing entry with the same name or ticker. If one exists, the new entry is not created; the existing entry is reviewed instead, and the check is recorded in the audit log (§9).
4. **Ticker verification at launch.** A ticker is verified at launch time against what exists on Pump.fun at that time. Registration does not reserve a ticker on any platform, and a registered ticker that is taken or conflicts at launch does not entitle the registry to use it. If a ticker cannot be used at launch, the coin does not launch under that ticker; the founder decides whether to rename, hold, or remove the entry, and the decision is logged.
5. **No entry is a promise to launch.** Registration records that a coin is planned and documented. It does not promise that the coin will launch, when it will launch, or that it will launch at all. A coin may remain Registered indefinitely, be held, or be removed under §7.
6. **Statuses.** Every entry carries exactly one status, from this closed list:
   - **Registered** — recorded in the registry; legal review not yet complete.
   - **Pending legal review** — the written per-asset Howey analysis has been requested and is not yet received or signed.
   - **Pending funding** — legal review has cleared for that coin; SOL funding in the wallet is not yet in place.
   - **Launched** — the coin has been created on Pump.fun after all three gates in §4 cleared. No Wave 1 coin holds this status as of the date of this document.
   
   No other status is used. No release date is stated in any entry. No entry carries a date for a future launch.
7. **What an entry contains.** Name, ticker, description as written, art source and file reference, stated purpose and use, issuer (ScrollVerse LLC), IP flag if any (§6), registration timestamp (§3), and current status. An entry does not contain a full design or blueprint (§6.4).
8. **Language rule.** No registry entry, and no copy derived from the registry, uses return, yield, appreciation, passive-income, floor-price, buyback, or profit-expectation language. The proceeds line in the coin descriptions — "A share of every transaction goes back to the company and to environmental causes" — is itself under Howey review (Question 3 in the Howey brief) and must not be used as a public donation or charitable claim until the attorney and, for any charitable claim, CPA review and written naming permission are in place. No unverified money or user figures appear in any entry.

## 3. Timestamping standard

The registry's purpose is a paper trail. A timestamp is only as good as its record, so the standard is:

### 3.1 What counts as a timestamp

A timestamp counts when it is produced by a system the founder does not control unilaterally and that preserves the record:

- **A GitHub commit** in a ScrollVerse repository, with its commit hash, repository name, and commit time; or
- **A published artifact** (a dated file in the workspace record or a published document) with its publication date and file location; or
- **Website publication** (for example, the ScrollVerse Atlas page carrying the coin) with its publication date and URL.

A private note, a spoken statement, or an undated file does not count as a timestamp.

### 3.2 What is recorded

For each registry event (registration, status change, gate clearance, dispute flag, removal, governance change), the audit log (§9) records:

- The date and time of the event;
- The coin and entry affected;
- What changed, in plain words;
- The author (who made the change — the founder, or a person acting on his express instruction for that change);
- The reason for the change;
- The timestamp evidence: the GitHub commit hash, artifact path, or publication URL that proves when the record was made.

### 3.3 How a timestamp is verified

Verification means a second person (or the founder on review) can follow the recorded evidence and reach the same date:

- For a GitHub commit: the commit hash is looked up in the named repository and the commit time confirmed.
- For an artifact: the file is opened at the recorded path and its date and contents confirmed.
- For website publication: the URL is opened and the published content and date confirmed.

If the evidence cannot be followed to the same date, the timestamp is treated as unverified, the entry is flagged in the audit log, and no release gate may be treated as cleared on the strength of that timestamp until it is verified.

## 4. Release criteria — the three gates, in order

No coin launches until **all three gates have cleared for that coin, in this order**. No gate may be skipped, combined, or waived. Clearance is per coin, not per wave.

### Gate 1 — Written per-asset Howey analysis clears for that coin

A licensed attorney provides a **written per-asset Howey analysis** for that specific coin, answering the 12 questions in the Howey brief (`~/workspace/legal/howey-brief-wave1-2026-11-18.md`): per-coin Howey conclusion (standalone asset and as offered and marketed), meme-coin guidance, the proceeds line, marketing rules, exemption paths if a coin is a security, Knicks IP, money transmission, charitable claims, platform risk, tax scope, launch sequence, and ongoing duties.

- The analysis must be in writing and signed by a licensed attorney. A verbal view, a consultation note, or an AI-generated draft does not clear this gate.
- The AI law system prepares: it may draft the analysis structure, the approved and prohibited language lists, and per-coin risk notes. A licensed human attorney reviews and signs. AI output is never a legal opinion, and no launch rests on it alone.
- The legal route is free lawyers first (Digital Currency and Ledger Defense Coalition; Rutgers Law clinics; Brooklyn Law BLIP Clinic; New Jersey Volunteer Lawyers for the Arts; Volunteer Lawyers for the Arts, New York; Harvard Law Cyberlaw Clinic), pay-after / deferred / instalment / crypto-payment second, and JD Woods Law at $349 per token as fallback — as set out in `~/workspace/legal/free-lawyers-howey-2026-11-18.md`. The route does not change the gate: whoever does the work, the written, signed, per-coin analysis is what clears it.
- If the attorney's conclusion for a coin is that it is a security, that it needs redesign, or that it must be held, the coin does not launch until the attorney's stated conditions are met and confirmed in writing.
- SVRS may clear while coins 2–7 are held for IP review, or all 7 may be held together — that is the attorney's per-coin recommendation to make (Howey brief Question 11). The registry follows the attorney's per-coin answer, not a blanket rule.

When Gate 1 clears for a coin, its status moves to **Pending funding** and the clearance (attorney, date, and the written analysis reference) is recorded in the audit log.

### Gate 2 — SOL funding in the wallet

SOL is deposited in the Pump.fun wallet (`FpxkpRuL6RT5a4P48Wy4FVsRSLdAFsMABMjYKsWvZr64`) in an amount sufficient to pay the creation cost for that coin (approximately 0.02 SOL in network fees per coin). The deposit is verified from the wallet itself before launch. A wallet balance of $0.00 does not clear this gate, and no coin is created until the funding is verified in place.

### Gate 3 — Founder sign-off on that coin

Chais Hill signs off on **that specific coin** — its name, ticker, description, art, and launch — after Gates 1 and 2 have cleared. Sign-off is recorded in the audit log with the date. Sign-off on one coin is not sign-off on another. Sign-off given before Gates 1 and 2 clear does not count and must be given again after they clear.

### 4.1 If any gate fails

If any gate fails or is not yet cleared, the coin stays at its current status (Pending legal review or Pending funding) and does not launch. A failed gate is recorded in the audit log with the reason. There is no partial launch, no launch "while waiting" on a gate, and no launch of a coin under a different name or ticker to get around a failed gate.

## 5. Merch-buyer head-start rules

Customers who buy merch get a head start on coins and rare NFTs before the drop. That head start is a token-as-reward / pre-sale structure, so it is governed by the same legal clearance as the coins themselves.

### 5.1 Only after legal clearance

The head start for a coin may **only** operate after the written per-asset Howey analysis has cleared **for that coin** (Gate 1, §4). Until then, the head start does not operate for that coin in any form. Activation of a head start for a coin requires founder approval (§8) and is recorded in the audit log.

### 5.2 Stage 1 only, until then

Until legal clearance, bundle incentives are **Stage 1 only**, as defined in `~/workspace/bundles/scrollverse-bundles-2026-11-18.md` §1:

- **Loyalty credits (ScrollCredits)** — non-transferable loyalty points earned by buying a bundle. No cash value, not redeemable for money, not a token, not an NFT, not tradeable. The word "credit" is used, never "coin", "token", or "share".
- **Early access to the launch announcement** — buyers are told first and get a priority window to **see** the launch when it happens. Early access is access to information and the launch page only.
- **Numbered runs** — the physical piece carries a hand-numbered edition. The number is a record of the run, not a promise of value.
- **Library access** — access to the ScrollVerse Library, as a distinct gated element (the Atlas reading rooms are already free to direct site visitors, so free access is never sold as a paid perk).
- **Earned roles** — a named role in the ScrollVerse community (for example "Collector") earned by buying the top tier. A role is recognition, not a financial interest, not governance, not a vote, not a share of anything.

### 5.3 What "head start" means — and does not mean

When, and only when, a coin's head start is activated after legal clearance:

- **It means:** merch buyers are told first, and get a priority window to see the launch when it happens.
- **It does not mean:** an allocation, a reservation, a guaranteed purchase, a pre-sale, a mint, or a token or NFT given as a reward for buying. No token or NFT is given, airdropped, reserved, or promised as a reward for buying merch, and no token sale, mint, or pre-sale is attached to any bundle or head start.
- **It does not mean** any return, yield, appreciation, passive-income, floor-price, or profit language. None of that language appears in any head-start copy.

If a coin does not clear legal review, or a launch is delayed, every bundle still delivers exactly what was bought (book, merch, poster) and the Stage-1 items that do not depend on the launch (numbered run, Library access, earned role, loyalty credits) still stand on their own.

## 6. IP and blueprint protection

### 6.1 Own IP vs third-party IP

- **Own IP:** Art, names, and likenesses created by Chais Hill himself — including the God Mode emblem (ScrollVerse, SVRS) — are his own IP. They carry no third-party IP flag.
- **Third-party IP:** Any name, logo, or likeness belonging to someone else — including a team, league, or living person — is third-party IP and is flagged on the coin's registry entry. The flag states what the third-party element is and that it is unresolved until the attorney's assessment is recorded.

### 6.2 The Knicks flag (coins 2–7)

Coins 2–7 (Cyborg Brunson / CYBORG, The Anchor / ANCHOR, Floor General / GENERAL, The Wing / WING, The Tower / TOWER, The Assassin / ASSASSIN) carry a **Knicks IP flag**: they use the New York Knicks name and logo elements and living players by name and likeness (Jalen Brunson, Patrick Ewing, Walt Frazier, OG Anunoby, Karl-Anthony Towns, Bernard King).

- The earlier "build as-is" decision covered **merch only**. It does not cover a token launch, and it is not treated as covering one.
- For tokens, the attorney is asked to assess trademark and right-of-publicity exposure for each coin and recommend one of three outcomes per coin: **license, redesign, or hold**.
- A flagged coin does not pass Gate 1 until the attorney's IP recommendation for that coin is recorded and, where the recommendation is license or redesign, the license is in place or the redesign is done and re-reviewed. Where the recommendation is hold, the coin stays Pending legal review and does not launch.
- The IP flag is stated on the registry entry and is not removed by the founder alone; it is removed only when the attorney's written recommendation and its follow-through are recorded.

### 6.3 What happens if a coin is challenged

If any person, team, league, platform, or authority challenges a coin — for IP, for securities, or for any other reason — the following applies:

1. The challenge is recorded in the audit log with the date, who raised it, and what was claimed.
2. The coin's entry is flagged as **disputed** (§7) and, if the challenge concerns the coin's right to exist or launch, the coin is **frozen**: it does not launch, and no head start operates for it, while the dispute is open.
3. The founder decides the response, with the attorney's advice where the challenge is legal in nature. The response may be to answer the challenge, seek a license, redesign the coin, hold it, or remove the entry. The decision and its reason are recorded in the audit log.
4. A disputed coin is not quietly renamed or re-entered under a new ticker to get around the challenge. Any new entry arising from a dispute is recorded as a new entry linked to the old one, with the dispute noted.

### 6.4 Blueprint protection

Detailed designs — blueprints — are **protected**. The registry records the **existence, authorship, and timestamps** of a design: that a design exists, who created it, and when it was recorded (under the timestamping standard in §3). The registry does **not** publish, reproduce, or distribute the full design itself.

- A blueprint is never attached to a public registry entry, coin page, or bundle listing.
- Access to a full blueprint is by the founder's decision only, and any access granted is recorded in the audit log (who, what, when, why).
- If a coin is challenged, the registry's timestamp record may be used to show existence, authorship, and date — the full design is disclosed only as the founder and his attorney decide.

### 6.5 Excluded material

The following are never registered, commercialised, or entered in the registry as coins or coin material: any material involving a child; the Code of Tawḥīd (free-only, never commercialised); screenshot-only works without originals; and any real photo of real people used without the basis to do so. If such material is submitted for registration, it is refused and the refusal is logged.

## 7. Dispute handling

### 7.1 Flagging

Any dispute about an entry — an IP challenge, a duplicate claim, a question about authorship or timestamp, or a legal challenge — is flagged on the entry as **disputed**, with the date, the person or party raising it, and the claim, recorded in the audit log. A flagged entry stays visible in the registry with its disputed status shown; it is not silently edited.

### 7.2 Freezing

While a dispute is open and concerns the coin's right to exist or launch, the entry is **frozen**: its status does not advance, it does not launch, and no head start operates for it. A frozen entry's status is recorded as its prior status plus the disputed flag, so the history is preserved. Freezing is not a finding against the coin; it is a pause while the dispute is decided.

### 7.3 Removal

An entry is removed only by the founder, and only for a recorded reason — for example, the attorney recommends it, the IP cannot be licensed or redesigned, the coin is withdrawn, or the entry is a duplicate. Removal is not deletion of history: the entry is marked **removed**, the reason and date are recorded in the audit log, and the record is retained. A removed entry is not re-entered without a new registration under §2, linked to the removed entry.

### 7.4 Who decides

The founder decides every dispute, with the attorney's advice where the dispute is legal in nature. No other person, platform, or system decides a dispute. The decision, the reason, and the date are recorded in the audit log.

## 9. Audit log requirement

Every change to the registry, to a coin, or to this governance document is recorded in an audit log. Every log entry records:

- **Date** — the date (and time, where known) of the change;
- **Author** — who made the change (the founder, or a person acting on his express instruction for that change);
- **Reason** — why the change was made, in plain words;
- **What changed** — the coin or entry affected and what was done;
- **Timestamp evidence** — the GitHub commit hash, artifact path, or publication URL that proves when the record was made (§3).

The log is append-only in practice: entries are not edited or deleted after the fact. A correction is made as a new entry that states what it corrects and why. The log is kept with the registry records and is available to the founder and his attorney on request.
